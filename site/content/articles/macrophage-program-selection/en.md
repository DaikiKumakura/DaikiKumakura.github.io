A decrease in M2 macrophages may sound favorable in cancer immunology: fewer immunosuppressive cells could allow T cells to act. But what were those cells actually doing?

The Immunity Perspective [Embracing macrophage complexity for research and translation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.09.015), published online on October 16, 2026, revisits frameworks based on fixed macrophage types or single markers. Cell states reflect developmental origin, tissue, prior stimuli and local environment. The proposed alternative describes combinations of gene-program activities and considers how interventions alter those combinations or transitions. This is a Perspective on research and translation, not an efficacy trial. I used its public abstract and overview to examine this framework ([1](#ref-1)).

The limitations of M1/M2 terminology are not new. The [2014 experimental guidelines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2014.06.008) already recommended specifying cell origin and stimulation conditions ([2](#ref-2)). In my reading, the Perspective matters because it moves beyond adding categories to asking which functions an intervention should preserve or change.

I reaggregated public human macrophage single-cell RNA data to compare two stimulation conditions that both fall under M2 and examine information lost when they are merged.

## Different stimuli under the same M2 label {#m2}

The input was [GSE199378](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE199378): blood monocytes from two healthy donors differentiated into macrophages and exposed to the stimuli below. The original study was published by Modak et al. in 2022; these measurements were not newly generated by the 2026 Perspective ([3](#ref-3)).

**Table 1.** Stimulation conditions and cell counts by donor.

| Original label used here | Stimulus | Donor 1 cells | Donor 2 cells |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| M1 | LPS + IFNγ | 3,884 | 4,537 |
| M2a | IL-4 + IL-13 | 1,407 | 4,756 |
| M2c | IL-10 | 3,631 | 3,014 |

There were 21,229 cells but only two independent donors. According to GEO, cells were differentiated with macrophage colony-stimulating factor, M-CSF, for six days and stimulated for 24 h. This timing differs from the paper's general culture procedure for functional experiments, so the RNA and functional measurements should not be treated as simultaneous readouts. LPS denotes lipopolysaccharide, IFNγ interferon gamma, and IL interleukin.

I used the original quality-controlled, scran-normalized log1p expression values without reclustering or additional batch correction. Many cells do not substitute for many donors.

## Keeping the two conditions separate {#_1}

Before opening the expression matrix, I specified six small panels containing 26 genes: IFN-related genes, antigen-processing genes, costimulatory ligands, checkpoint ligands, type 2-related genes and uptake receptors.

These are panels I selected for comparison, not a reconstruction of the 2026 Perspective's program list. They neither identify new regulatory circuits nor assume that the panels act independently.

**Table 2.** Prespecified comparison panels.

| Panel | Genes used |
| --- | --- |
| IFN-related | STAT1, IRF1, GBP1, CXCL9, CXCL10, IDO1 |
| Antigen processing | HLA-A, HLA-B, B2M, TAP1, TAP2, PSMB8 |
| Costimulatory ligands | CD80, CD86, ICOSLG |
| Checkpoint ligands | CD274, PDCD1LG2 |
| Type 2-related | CCL17, CCL18, CCL22, ALOX15, TGM2 |
| Uptake receptors | MRC1, CD163, MERTK, MSR1 |

I calculated mean log1p expression for each donor–stimulus combination, standardized each gene using the six sample means with equal weights, and averaged the resulting z scores within each panel. Equal weighting prevents samples with more cells from dominating the reference scale.

$$
S_{ds,P}=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{g\in P}\frac{\bar{x}_{ds,g}-\mu_g}{\sigma_g}
$$

Here, \(d\) denotes donor and \(s\) stimulus. For each gene, \(\mu_g\) and \(\sigma_g\) are the mean and standard deviation of the six sample means; the standard-deviation denominator is 6. Zero represents this dataset's average, not absence of function. Because all samples define the scale, this is descriptive visualization rather than prediction for an unseen donor.

![Six gene panels across two donors and three stimuli](shared/macrophage-program-selection/figures/program-panels.png)

**Figure 1.** Mean panel z scores across two donors and three stimulation conditions, standardized with equal weights for six samples. Scores are not absolute functional activities.

For M2a minus M2c, costimulatory-ligand scores were higher by 1.14 in donor 1 and 0.95 in donor 2. Type 2-related scores were higher by 1.33 and 1.92, whereas uptake-receptor differences were −0.59 and −1.01. M2a was not uniformly more activated across panels.

Checkpoint-ligand scores were also higher in M2a, by 0.23 and 0.14. Higher costimulation-related RNA did not mean lower inhibition-related RNA. These patterns cannot determine the direction of an antitumor effect.

Removing one gene at a time preserved the direction of these four panel differences in every case. IFN-related differences were smaller and reversed in 2 of 12 cases. There is little basis for emphasizing the sign of such small differences. The distributed tables also retain unstandardized gene means and detection fractions.

## An unchanged M2 fraction can conceal a changing composition {#m2_1}

Consider a hypothetical pool combining each donor's M2a and M2c samples. If the M2a fraction is \(p\), its mean panel score is

$$
S_{\mathrm{M2}}(p)=pS_{\mathrm{M2a}}+(1-p)S_{\mathrm{M2c}}
$$

This is a static reweighting of two observed conditions, not an experiment changing cells with a drug or a model of temporal evolution.

![Static mixture reweighting and donor-specific gene differences](shared/macrophage-program-selection/figures/mixture-and-markers.png)

**Figure 2.** Left: static reweighting of observed M2a and M2c conditions. Right: donor-specific differences in mean gene expression. The figure estimates neither state transitions nor intervention effects.

All cells in this hypothetical pool are M2 at every \(p\). Nevertheless, composition alone moves the costimulatory-ligand score by 1.14 in donor 1 and 0.95 in donor 2. A record stating no change in the M2 fraction would lose this difference.

MRC1 in the right-hand panel encodes CD206, whereas CD163 encodes a different receptor. Both appear in descriptions of M2, but their differences have opposite signs in these conditions. Even the combined uptake-receptor panel does not preserve every single-marker difference. Replacing a label with a panel is not a complete solution.

Before and after treatment, both composition and expression within a state may change. Separating them requires comparisons maintaining state definitions or experiments that track changes. Cross-sectional measurements under different stimuli here do not show that M2c cells became M2a cells.

## Functional measurements beyond RNA {#rna}

The original 2022 paper examined antigen cross-presentation as well as expression: presentation of internalized antigen to T cells through major histocompatibility complex class I. With soluble long TyrD peptide, M2a showed stronger cross-presentation and IFNγ responses from TyrD-specific T58 cells than M1 or M2c. Comparable responses were not clear with antigen from dead cells. These measurements appear in [Figure 4 of the original paper](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/155022) ([3](#ref-3)).

This conditionality matters. Calling M2a antitumor would merely create another fixed label. Antigen form, presentation process and measured T-cell response must remain part of the description. The two RNA donors are not the donor count of the functional experiments. This analysis does not regress functional effect sizes on RNA differences.

Surface markers may provide an entry point for intervention without identifying cells that all have the same function. These two RNA donors do not quantitatively separate changes in cell abundance, state and antigen-presentation function. M1/M2 labels alone do not determine which functions remain or disappear.

## Limitations {#_2}

This analysis describes cultured cells from two healthy donors under three stimulation conditions. It includes neither unstimulated M0 cells, tissue-resident cells nor dose or time series. It estimates no population-wide donor effect, treatment benefit, toxicity or transition rate. I report neither cell-level p values nor population confidence intervals.

The layer named `counts` in the public h5ad file was identical to `X` and did not contain integer unique molecular identifier counts. I therefore used it as documented normalized log1p expression, rather than assuming that its name indicated raw counts or fitting a count-based differential-expression model.
