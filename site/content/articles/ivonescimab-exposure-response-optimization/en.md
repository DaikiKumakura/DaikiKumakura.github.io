Reference date: the facts in this article reflect public information as of 5 November 2026.

## Abstract

Published papers on ivonescimab (AK112), a bispecific antibody against PD-1 (programmed cell death-1) and VEGF-A (vascular endothelial growth factor A), report scattered aggregate values: group-level pharmacokinetics (PK), PD-1 receptor occupancy, VEGF change, and counts of objective response (ORR) and treatment-related adverse events (TRAE) by regimen. This article asks, with a reproducible analysis, how far these aggregates alone can quantitatively support a comparison of dose and dosing interval. No subject-level data and no digitised figure values were used.

The Phase I group summaries (3–30 mg/kg) were described by a linear two-compartment model, and the dose-proportionality slope was close to 1 (1.04 for the area under the concentration-time curve to infinity, AUC0-∞; 90% interval 0.95–1.12). The gain of the two-compartment model came from seven first-interval trough concentrations and depended on the assumed window for the apparent half-life (t1/2). For the same weekly intensity of 10 mg/kg, 20 mg/kg Q2W (every 2 weeks) and 30 mg/kg Q3W (every 3 weeks) have equal mean steady-state concentration by model structure; the peak with 30 mg/kg Q3W was predicted to be about 1.34-fold (95% credible interval for parameter uncertainty 1.32–1.36) and the trough 0.58-fold (one compartment) to 0.78-fold (two compartments, 0.63–0.85). PD-1 occupancy reached about 93–98% at 3 mg/kg on Day 2 and Day 8, and the pre-dose baseline varied from 23.6% to 56.2%, so dose levels could not be discriminated. In the chemotherapy-combination Phase II (10 versus 20 mg/kg Q3W), the log odds ratio of response per doubling was −0.17 (95% credible interval −0.99 to 0.64; odds ratio 0.37–1.9), which settles neither direction nor size. The Phase III results for 20 mg/kg Q3W are drug comparisons, not dose comparisons. The optimisation of dose and interval cannot be decided from public data.

## Introduction

Ivonescimab is a tetravalent bispecific antibody that binds PD-1 and VEGF-A. In vitro experiments with recombinant proteins report that PD-1 binding increases more than 18-fold when VEGF is present ([1](#ref-1)). The specific values under cooperative conditions appear only in a figure of the original paper and were not estimated here.

In humans, a Chinese Phase I study (AK112-102) evaluated 3–30 mg/kg Q2W and 10 and 20 mg/kg Q3W ([2](#ref-2)), and an Australian Phase Ia study (AK112-101) evaluated 0.3–30 mg/kg Q2W ([3](#ref-3)). A Phase Ib/II study (AK112-202, reported under the name HARMONi-5) compared 10 mg/kg Q3W, 20 mg/kg Q2W, 20 mg/kg Q3W and 30 mg/kg Q3W in a non-randomised design in immunotherapy-naive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) ([4](#ref-4)). A Phase II study with chemotherapy (AK112-201) allocated 10 and 20 mg/kg Q3W sequentially ([5](#ref-5)). In Phase III, HARMONi-2 compared ivonescimab 20 mg/kg Q3W monotherapy with pembrolizumab ([6](#ref-6)). HARMONi-6 compared ivonescimab 20 mg/kg Q3W plus paclitaxel and carboplatin with tislelizumab plus the same chemotherapy in squamous disease; progression-free survival was reported in 2025 ([7](#ref-7)) and overall survival in 2026 ([8](#ref-8), [9](#ref-9)).

The regulatory status as of 5 November 2026, from company primary sources, is as follows. The Akeso product page lists approvals in China in May 2024 (EGFR [epidermal growth factor receptor]-mutant non-squamous disease after EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor [TKI], with chemotherapy) and April 2025 (PD-L1 [programmed death-ligand 1]-positive first-line monotherapy) ([10](#ref-10)), and Akeso announced the approval for first-line squamous disease with chemotherapy on 12 August 2026 ([11](#ref-11)). The same announcement records that HARMONi-6 progression-free survival was presented at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) 2025 meeting ([11](#ref-11)). Overall survival was reported at the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) 2026 meeting ([9](#ref-9)). In the United States, Summit Therapeutics states that the Biologics License Application (BLA) for HARMONi (EGFR-mutant, after third-generation TKI, non-squamous) was accepted in January 2026, that the Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) goal date is 14 November 2026, and that there was no approval as of 5 November 2026 ([12](#ref-12)). The goal date precedes the scheduled publication date of this article, so a decision may have been made by the time of reading. The database of the Chinese National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) and US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) documents were not checked, so these are company statements.

The approved dose is mainly 20 mg/kg Q3W. The question is how far published aggregates support a quantitative comparison of dose and dosing interval across the tested regimens. Five sub-questions were posed. Q1: Can PD-1 occupancy discriminate dose saturation? Q2: Does VEGF change depend on the dosing interval? Q3: Is the clinical dose response identifiable by regimen? Q4: Is the exposure-toxicity relationship identifiable? Q5: For 20 mg/kg Q3W, what is supported, not supported and undecidable? Dose recommendation is not an aim.

## Methods

Dose is in mg/kg, volumes are per body weight (L/kg), and clearance (CL) is in L/day/kg. One- (1c) and two-compartment (2c) linear models were compared with a 1.5-hour infusion input (60–120 minutes is reported, so 1.0 and 2.0 hours were also checked).

\[ V_1\frac{dC_1}{dt}=R(t)-CL\,C_1-Q\,(C_1-C_2),\qquad V_2\frac{dC_2}{dt}=Q\,(C_1-C_2) \]

Here \(R(t)\) is the infusion rate (mg/kg/day), \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are the central and peripheral concentrations (µg/mL), \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) are the central and peripheral volumes, and \(Q\) is the inter-compartmental clearance. The 1c model is the case \(Q=0\). The closed-form solution was verified against numerical ODE integration and a fixed-step RK4 scheme (Figure 1).

![Figure 1: Model structure and its link to the aggregate values](shared/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization/figures/fig1_model_structure.png)

Figure 1: Mechanistic context (A), linear PK model (B), and public aggregate values linked to the model (C).

The calibration put single-dose maximum concentration (Cmax), AUC0-∞, apparent terminal half-life (t1/2) and the trough at the end of the first interval (the Day 15 or Day 22 pre-dose value of Table S7) into the likelihood at the same time. These come from the same subjects and are correlated, and in 1c the t1/2 is determined by CL and V1, so it is a double count. To dampen this overconfidence, summary \(m\) of group \(g\) was modelled as

\[ \log y_{gm}=\log f_m(\theta;d_g,\tau_g)+\eta_{gm}+\varepsilon_{gm},\quad \varepsilon_{gm}\sim N(0,\mathrm{se}_{gm}^2),\ \eta_{gm}\sim N(0,\sigma_m^2) \]

where \(\theta\) is the parameter vector, \(d_g\) the dose, \(\tau_g\) the dosing interval, and \(f_m\) the model prediction. The se is the coefficient of variation divided by √n from the reported SD, and \(\sigma_m\) is heterogeneity between dose groups (population, sampling and assay differences), not inter-individual variability. The model t1/2 was computed from concentrations on days 3–14 (Q3W sampling extends to day 21, but the same window was used for all groups). CL, Vz (the NCA volume of distribution), mean concentration and the accumulation ratio (Rac) were not used because they derive from other summaries. The marginal likelihood was maximised, and a posterior was obtained by adaptive Metropolis sampling under weakly informative priors (one chain, 70,000 iterations, 15,000 burn-in, thinning 20). Identifiability of the 2c model was checked by profile likelihood, and AIC and the small-sample AICc (28 observations) were compared. Steady-state summaries (Cmax,ss, minimum concentration Cmin,ss, AUC0-τ,ss, Rac), the time course of Table S7 troughs, and the Phase Ia study were not used for calibration and served only for comparison with predictions.

The regimens were 10, 20 and 30 mg/kg Q3W, 20 mg/kg Q2W, and a hypothetical 15 mg/kg Q3W. Their mean dose intensities are 3.33, 6.67, 10.00, 10.00 and 5.00 mg/kg/week. A common 42-day window (three Q2W or two Q3W intervals) was used to compare mean concentrations over the same window. Inter-individual variability was a conditional scenario input spanning the reported CL coefficients of variation (15.4–27.0%) (CL coefficient of variation 15, 25 and 35%): lognormal variation in CL and central volume with correlation 0 or 0.5. No covariance was estimated.

PD-1 occupancy (RO) was compared as group-by-time means against the concentration of the typical subject from the model. With the pre-dose Day 1 value \(a_g\) (%) as an assay offset and \(b\) the upper limit of occupancy (%),

\[ \mathrm{RO}_{gt}=a_g+(b-a_g)\,\frac{C_{gt}}{C_{gt}+EC_{50}} \]

was fitted by weighted least squares with a floor on the standard error of 1, 3 and 5 percentage points (pp, differences between percentages), and the profile of \(EC_{50}\) was examined. The in vitro KD was converted to µg/mL assuming a molar mass of 150–200 kDa (context only).

Response and adverse events were summarised with Beta-Binomial posteriors under a Beta(2, 2) prior, with prior sensitivity (Jeffreys, uniform, informative, sceptical). The Phase II dose slope was a log odds ratio per doubling from a stratified logistic model in which cohort-specific intercepts were integrated numerically. For Phase I adverse events, the slope of a logistic regression on log2 dose was obtained as a posterior on a grid. Because regimen-level numbers are not available for the Phase Ib/II study (C), only the percentages in the abstract are shown, and no counts, intervals or slope were computed. Every number comes from the output of `analysis.py` (`results/`), with random seed 20261105.

## Results

### Pharmacokinetics

Dose proportionality from group means for Q2W 3–30 mg/kg gave a Cmax slope of 0.99 (90% interval 0.87–1.11) and an AUC0-∞ slope of 1.04 (0.95–1.12), not in conflict with the reported individual-level slopes (0.96 and 1.04) ([2](#ref-2), [Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1002/cam4.70653)) (`pk_dose_proportionality.csv`). Dose-normalised values scattered by about −15% to +30% around the group average (Figure 2), and this heterogeneity was absorbed by \(\sigma_m\) (0.07–0.13).

![Figure 2: Group PK summaries and model](shared/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization/figures/fig2_pk_dose_groups.png)

Figure 2: Single-dose Cmax and AUC0-∞ divided by dose (top and middle), and the trough at the end of the first interval (bottom). Points are group means; error bars are ±1.96 SE (standard error). Lines are the 1c and 2c fits.

The 2c model fitted better than 1c (AIC difference −8.7, AICc difference −5.1, AICc weight 0.93). Central CL was 0.0065 L/day/kg (95% credible interval 0.0057–0.0071), \(V_1\) 0.051 L/kg (0.046–0.055), \(Q\) 0.0058 L/day/kg (0.0023–0.0096) and \(V_2\) 0.039 L/kg (0.010–0.072). The profile-likelihood 95% intervals of \(Q\) and \(V_2\) were also finite. The terminal half-life of 2c is 12.8 days, and the apparent half-life over days 3–14 is 6.3 days, which does not contradict the reported NCA t1/2 (5.0–7.3 days) ([2](#ref-2), Table 3).

This gain is fragile. First, it is carried by the seven first-interval troughs: removing them eliminated it (AIC difference +1.1) and left the upper bound of \(Q\) undefined. Second, removing t1/2 moved \(Q\) about 4-fold to 0.024, and a t1/2 window of 3–21 days gave \(Q\) 0.0027 and an AICc difference of +0.6, so the advantage of 2c disappeared. Third, faster elimination at low concentration (target-mediated elimination or anti-drug antibodies) could explain the troughs as well as a peripheral compartment. Fourth, 2c missed part of the steady-state checks below. The 2c model is therefore taken as the primary model because it describes the seven troughs best, 1c is kept as a structural alternative, and ratios between regimens are given as a range over the two. MCMC diagnostics for 2c were split R-hat 1.000–1.005 and effective sample size 376–1,261 of 2,750 draws. Downstream analyses used 400–500 draws spaced evenly over the whole chain. Scaling the prior standard deviations by 0.5 and 2 changed the lower credible bound of \(Q\) to 0.0031–0.0020 and that of the trough ratio to 0.69–0.62, without changing the conclusion. The infusion length (1.0, 1.5, 2.0 hours) did not change it either.

The AUC0-∞ used in calibration contains NCA extrapolation and is not necessarily the truth. If the 2c terminal half-life (12.8 days) is longer than the NCA t1/2 (5–7 days), the Q2W AUC0-∞ is underestimated and the calibrated CL may be about 10% too high. Increasing the observed AUC0-∞ by 10% lowered CL to 0.0060 (about 7% lower), and excluding AUC0-∞ gave a 2c CL of 0.0049.

Ratios of reported steady-state values (Table 3) to predictions under 2c were a geometric mean of 1.06 for Cmax,ss (range 0.87–1.37), 0.88 for Cmin,ss (0.61–1.33) and 1.10 for AUC0-τ,ss (0.92–1.43), with no large departure (Figure 3, top); the check has low power because the combined heterogeneity and SE intervals are wide. The reported AUC0-τ,ss was 1.14-fold (1.02–1.27) the single-dose AUC0-∞, and CLss was 0.87-fold (0.77–0.97) CL, away from the 1.0 predicted by a linear time-invariant model. Explanations of comparable plausibility are a time-dependent decline in CL, disease or anti-drug antibody effects, and bias from NCA extrapolation: if extrapolation is insufficient and t1/2 is underestimated, AUC0-∞ is underestimated and the ratio exceeds 1 (the size of this bias depends on the sampling design and was not estimated here). The Table S7 trough time course had an average ratio of about 1.0 across groups under 2c, but the late values (Day 197) at 20 mg/kg Q2W reached 2.1 times the prediction (Figure 3, middle). Because n per time point is unknown, selection through dropout cannot be assessed.

![Figure 3: Accumulation predictions and comparison with the Phase Ia study](shared/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization/figures/fig3_accumulation_external.png)

Figure 3: Ratio of reported steady-state NCA values to predictions (top), trough time course at 20 mg/kg (middle), and comparison with the Phase Ia study (bottom). A ratio near 1 means agreement.

The values of the Phase Ia study ([supplementary tables of JITC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2023-008037); [3](#ref-3)) at 10, 20 and 30 mg/kg Q2W were not used for calibration and were 1.01–1.30 times the prediction for single-dose Cmax and AUC0-∞ and 0.95–1.45 times for the three steady-state summaries (2c; geometric means 1.15 and 1.29). At the shared doses, Cmax and AUC0-∞ were a geometric mean of 1.22-fold (range 1.00–1.39) higher than in the Phase I study (`pk_B_over_A.csv`). Because the two studies differ in population, sampling, assay and NCA conventions, this is not an independent validation of the model but an estimate of the between-study difference in exposure level.

### Steady-state exposure by regimen

Under linear PK, equal mean dose intensity gives equal mean steady-state concentration. For 20 mg/kg Q2W and 30 mg/kg Q3W the mean concentration over the 42-day window was identical in both models (221 µg/mL under 2c). This is a consequence of the model structure, not a discovery. The difference is in the shape of the concentration (Table 2, Figure 4).

### PD-1 occupancy and VEGF

The pre-dose (Day 1) occupancy was 23.6–56.2% as a group mean (mean within-group SD 27.6 pp) and was strongly correlated with dose rank (Spearman rank correlation 0.93, seven groups). Post-dose occupancy was 93.1–99.8% at Day 8 and 92.1–95.6% as the time-point mean of pre-dose values, with a mean within-group SD of 6.1 pp (Figure 5; calculated here from the group means of [Table 5](https://doi.org/10.1002/cam4.70653); [2](#ref-2)). At 3 mg/kg, occupancy was 97.5% on Day 2 and 93.1% on Day 8, but later pre-dose means scattered over 88.3–100%. The dose slope at Day 8 was small, 5.5 pp per decade (95% interval 1.2–9.8) in the five Q2W groups, and does not include 0. Because the correlation between the baseline and Day 8 across groups is high (0.76), a dose effect cannot be separated from baseline differences in the assay. The lowest model concentration (13.2 µg/mL) is 270–360 times the converted in vitro KD (0.037–0.049 µg/mL), and occupancy under 1:1 binding exceeds 99.6%. Occupancy is therefore near its plateau (above 90%) from 3 mg/kg, and a small dose trend is not excluded. The assay may also not reach 100%.

![Figure 5: PD-1 occupancy](shared/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization/figures/fig5_pd1_occupancy.png)

Figure 5: Baseline and post-dose occupancy by group (top), occupancy against model concentration (middle), and the \(EC_{50}\) profile (bottom). RO, PD-1 occupancy.

The upper limit of the 95% interval of the occupancy \(EC_{50}\) varied 4-fold, 0.40, 1.26 and 1.58 µg/mL, for standard-error floors of 1, 3 and 5 pp. The chi-square per degrees of freedom was 251/58, 81/58 and 35/58, so the fit is poor at floors of 1 and 3 pp. The lower limit was defined only for the floor of 3 pp (0.10 µg/mL). The upper limit of \(EC_{50}\) is conditional on a hyperbolic model, the cap on \(b\), group-specific offsets, and treating repeated measures from the same patients as independent points, and the interval is optimistic. Occupancy values for 0.3 and 1 mg/kg appear only in a figure of the Phase Ia study, with no numerical table.

VEGF change ([Table 5](https://doi.org/10.1002/cam4.70653)) was −88.6% to −55.4% at end of infusion (group means) and −55.8% to −19.6% pre-dose, showing recovery (Figure 6). The relation between pre-dose change and the predicted pre-dose concentration had a slope of 3.6 pp per decade (bootstrap 95% interval over groups −44 to 34; 47 points, seven groups), so no concentration dependence was detected. The width of this interval is comparable to the whole observed range of change (about 36 pp) and is uninformative (a bootstrap over seven groups may under-cover). The Q3W minus Q2W difference at the same dose (mean of pre-dose values) was 31 pp (−12 to 81) at 10 mg/kg and 16 pp (−20 to 53) at 20 mg/kg, both including 0. This interval treats repeated time points as independent, and it is confounded by the design difference that Q3W pre-dose samples are taken 21 days after dosing and Q2W samples 14 days. The +162% after the first 10 mg/kg Q3W dose is explained in the source by one subject whose baseline was below the lower limit of quantification. The text does not state whether free or total VEGF was measured, and absolute concentrations are not reported. The sign of the post-dose decrease is consistent with free VEGF (or an assay subject to antibody interference), but this cannot be decided. Mean change in groups with a baseline near the lower limit of quantification (15.6 pg/mL) is dominated by a few subjects. Suppression of tissue VEGF and the degree of cooperative binding cannot be estimated.

### Response rates and adverse events

In the chemotherapy-combination Phase II (D) ([5](#ref-5), [Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102106)), responses at 10 and 20 mg/kg Q3W were 10/19 and 13/24 in cohort 1, 8/10 and 5/9 in cohort 2, and 4/10 and 4/10 in cohort 3 (denominator: the full analysis set, FAS). The 20 versus 10 mg/kg difference (posterior mean, 95% credible interval) was +1.4 pp (−25.6 to +28.3) in cohort 1, −17.5 pp (−51.2 to +17.7) in cohort 2 and 0 pp (−35.3 to +35.2) in cohort 3 (Figure 8). The common slope across cohort strata was a log odds ratio of −0.17 per doubling (−0.99 to 0.64); the odds ratio of 0.84 (0.37–1.91) excludes neither a halving nor a near doubling of the odds. The prior standard deviation was 1 and the posterior 0.42, so the data reduced the standard deviation by 58%, but the interval still spans both directions. Heterogeneity between cohorts was not detected (Cochran Q 0.97 on 2 degrees of freedom, p = 0.61; I² 0%), but with three cohorts the power is low, and the cohort differences (+1.4, −17.5, 0 pp) do not exclude an interaction. The random-effects pooled value was −0.18 (−1.04 to 0.68). From a reference response rate of 50%, the difference at 20 mg/kg was −4.2 pp (−22.9 to 15.6), and the probability that the difference is under 10 pp was 0.63. Extrapolating hypothetically from 20 to 30 mg/kg gives a change of −2.5 pp (−14.1 to +9.3), but 30 mg/kg Q3W is not in D. Allocation was sequential and non-randomised within cohorts, so dose is confounded with enrolment period.

![Figure 8: Response rate intervals](shared/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization/figures/fig8_orr_intervals.png)

Figure 8: Posterior mean and 95% credible interval of response in D (top), the 20 versus 10 mg/kg difference (middle), and the percentages in the HARMONi-5 abstract (bottom, no n).

The response rates in C ([abstract of JTO 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtho.2023.10.014); [4](#ref-4)) are 33.3% for 10 mg/kg Q3W, 52.6% for 20 mg/kg Q2W, 60.0% for 20 mg/kg Q3W and 75.0% for 30 mg/kg Q3W (PD-L1-positive first line, 67 patients). Because regimen-level numbers are not available, no counts, intervals or dose-response slope were computed, and this is a non-randomised comparison of dose cohorts within a single-arm study, not a randomised dose comparison.

Grade 3+ TRAE in the Phase I study (A, monotherapy) ([2](#ref-2), [Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1002/cam4.70653)) were 3, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1 and 2 of 10, 10, 9, 12, 6, 6 and 6 patients (3, 5, 10, 20 and 30 mg/kg Q2W, then 10 and 20 mg/kg Q3W). The slope of the five Q2W groups on log2 dose was an odds ratio of 1.09 per doubling (0.63–1.94; probability of a positive slope 0.63), and 0.89 (0.34–2.31) in the Phase Ia study at 10, 20 and 30 mg/kg (6/19, 5/18, 2/7) ([3](#ref-3)). In the chemotherapy combination D (safety set, cohorts pooled) ([5](#ref-5), [Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102106)), the rates were 9/40 (22.5%) and 13/43 (30.2%), a difference of +6.9 pp (−11.6 to +25.1; probability of positive 0.77). The ORR denominator (FAS) and the TRAE denominator (safety set) differ. Changing the prior among five choices moved small groups (for 3/10 at 3 mg/kg, 0.14–0.61 with Beta(2, 2) versus 0.08–0.51 with the sceptical Beta(1, 4)). Beta(2, 2) is centred on 50%, which is high for toxicity rates of 10–30%, and pulls small groups upward. The conclusion that intervals are wide did not change. In the seven groups of A, using dose, mean concentration, peak or trough as the covariate gave an AIC about 1.7 worse than the null model (log-likelihood gain 0.12–0.14; AIC 68.4 versus 66.7), and group-level exposure is strongly correlated with dose (correlation 0.93–1.0), so there was no detectable relationship between Grade 3+ TRAE and dose or any exposure metric (Figure 9, `er_A_trae_covariate_comparison.csv`). Treatment and observation durations differ by group, so these proportions are not rates per exposure time.

As Phase III context, Grade 3+ TRAE in HARMONi-2 (20 mg/kg Q3W monotherapy versus pembrolizumab) were 58/197 (29%) and 31/199 (16%) ([6](#ref-6)), and in HARMONi-6 (chemotherapy combination versus tislelizumab combination) 184/266 (69%) and 156/265 (59%) at the overall survival analysis ([8](#ref-8)). The counts are those of the abstracts; the differences of +13.6 pp (5.5–21.7) and +10.2 pp (2.1–18.2) are Beta(2, 2) posterior values calculated here. These are cumulative proportions over different follow-up and exposure durations. They are drug comparisons, and the toxicity of chemotherapy combinations is not transferred to monotherapy. The HARMONi-2 overall survival was presented in September 2026 as a hazard ratio of 0.73 (0.57–0.95) with median 30.8 versus 22.6 months ([14](#ref-14)). The data cut-off of 20 August 2026 comes from news summaries, the company release gives no date ([15](#ref-15), [16](#ref-16)), and no peer-reviewed paper was found. Grade 3+ TRAE at the updated cut-off were not found. The progression-free survival analysis (cut-off 29 January 2024, median follow-up 8.67 months) is based on 2024 slides from the World Conference on Lung Cancer (WCLC) ([13](#ref-13)).

## Discussion

Q1 (occupancy): Occupancy is near its plateau (above 90%) from 3 mg/kg and could not discriminate dose. No numerical table is published for the low doses (0.3 and 1 mg/kg) needed for discrimination. The small Day 8 trend cannot be separated from the baseline offset. Q2 (VEGF): No interval dependence was detected, and whether the assay measures free or total VEGF is unknown. Q3 (response dose-response): Even for the two chemotherapy-combination doses the odds ratio of 0.37–1.9 settles neither direction nor size, and monotherapy has no regimen-level numbers. Q4 (exposure and toxicity): No detectable relationship exists, and exposure metrics cannot be distinguished because of collinearity.

Q5 (20 mg/kg Q3W): Supported are that occupancy is in its plateau range even at the trough of this dose, and that this dose was evaluated as a drug comparison in HARMONi-2 and HARMONi-6. As a consequence of linearity, the steady-state Cavg is twice that of 10 mg/kg Q3W and about 1.3 times that of 15 mg/kg Q3W. Not supported is a dose comparison in which 20 mg/kg Q3W is more effective or safer than other doses or intervals; the public data contain no such comparison. Undecidable are differences in efficacy and safety at higher doses (such as 30 mg/kg Q3W) or at the same intensity with a different interval (20 mg/kg Q2W). The difference in exposure shape between the latter two (peak about 1.34-fold, trough 0.58–0.78-fold) can be predicted, but whether it translates into a clinical difference cannot be estimated. The trough ratio depends on the structural choice of a peripheral compartment, which does not move the ranking but moves the size.

## Limitations

The published values are group means (SD) and counts only; no inter-individual covariance and no subject-level exposure-response pairs can be built. \(\sigma_m\) is a maximum-likelihood value from 4–7 points, and the overconfidence of a likelihood that ignores within-group correlation is only dampened. Dose proportionality was estimated from five group means (3 degrees of freedom). Because n per time point is unknown, the standard errors of Table S7 are lower bounds. The variability scenarios are assumptions. The ratio of fitted CL times body weight to the reported CL in the Phase I study was 0.75–1.23 (geometric mean 0.91); there is no unit error, but differences of ±25% remain from mean versus median and NCA bias. D is a sequential, non-randomised allocation, and its adverse events are pooled across cohorts. Parts of Phase III rest on abstracts, conference slides and company releases, and the HARMONi-2 overall survival update was not checked in a peer-reviewed paper. The Phase I population is multi-tumour, with one (A) and two (B) NSCLC patients. The comparison with the Phase Ia study is not an independent validation. The regulatory statements rest on company primary sources, and the NMPA database was not checked. Figure 11 summarises the data that were not available.

## Conclusion

For regimens of equal dose intensity such as 20 mg/kg Q2W and 30 mg/kg Q3W, the published aggregates supported, through the structure of linear PK, a conditional prediction of equal mean concentration, about 1.3-fold higher peak and a 0.6–0.8-fold trough. Occupancy and VEGF change did not discriminate dose and interval, and the dose response of response rate and toxicity was identifiable only within wide ranges. The Phase III results for 20 mg/kg Q3W contain no comparison with other doses or intervals, so the optimisation of dose and interval cannot be decided from public data.

## Data and code availability

The inputs are values transcribed from the tables of the original papers. The Phase I paper (A) in Cancer Medicine is CC BY, and its transcribed tables are included in the distribution (transcribed and reformatted, so a modified version). The Phase Ia study (B) is CC BY-NC, the chemotherapy-combination Phase II (D) and the non-clinical binding paper (G) are CC BY-NC-ND, and the abstracts, conference materials and company releases (C and E) are the works of their rights holders, so these transcribed tables are not distributed. Result tables derived from these values, such as the external comparison with B and the posteriors for B, C, D and Phase III (`pk_external_B.csv`, `pk_B_over_A.csv`, `rates_B_*`, `rates_C_*`, `rates_D_*`, `rates_phase3_*`), are also excluded from the distribution and are regenerated when the inputs are re-extracted and the code is run. The re-extraction procedure is in `README-public.md`. The analysis (`analysis.py`, `stages_*.py`, `pkmodel.py`, `pkfit.py`, `rates.py`), figures (`figures.py`), tests (`test_analysis.py`, 20 functions, 21 cases), the other result CSV/JSON files and figures are provided. The random seed is 20261105, with Python 3.12, NumPy 2.2, SciPy 1.14, pandas 2.2 and matplotlib 3.9. After re-extracting B, C, D, E and G, the full rerun takes about 4–5 minutes, and the results were reproduced byte for byte. With the distributed input (A) alone, the steps that read these inputs (external comparison with the Phase Ia study, occupancy fitting [G], response and adverse events, data-gap table) cannot be run.

The full re-run materials are in the [companion archive](shared/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization/ivonescimab-exposure-response-optimization-companion.zip).
