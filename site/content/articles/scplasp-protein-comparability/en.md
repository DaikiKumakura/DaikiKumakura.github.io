Identifying approximately 5600 protein groups from a single cell expands the observable proteome. The new scpLASP report is an example of that progress. For drug research, however, the question extends beyond identification depth: can the same proteins be compared quantitatively between treated and control cells?

There is a gap between those quantities. Even if 6000 items are detected in each cell, they need not be the same 6000 items. I first separate the measurement conditions in the new report, then use a small calculation to distinguish per-cell detection from shared observations.

## Scope and sources

The subject is the preprint by Okuda et al., posted on 29 October 2026. I accessed its original abstract but not the full text, supplementary tables or raw data. Actual cross-cell missingness and power to detect drug responses are therefore not evaluated here. I also consulted the 2024 scpLASP study for its library comparison. Sources were checked on 1 November 2026 ([1](#ref-1), [2](#ref-2)).

The later numbers are an original hypothetical calculation of detection probabilities. They are not estimates inferred from the new study's median of 5655.

## The conditions behind 5655

The new report describes HEK293-derived Expi293F single cells, with a median of 5655 identified protein groups per cell. It uses an empirical spectral library generated from 20-cell samples and a 5.5 min liquid-chromatography (LC) gradient. Effective throughput, including sample loading, is reported as 135 samples/day ([1](#ref-1)).

A protein group is a unit grouped by the analysis. The count does not mean 5655 distinct functions or activities were measured individually. Identification also differs from quantitative measurement with sufficient precision for a comparison.

In particular, the reported coefficients of variation (CVs), 9.20% and 6.99%, concern 100 pg and 250 pg HeLa digests. They are not variability across different Expi293F single cells ([1](#ref-1)). Those CVs cannot simply be inserted into a sample-size calculation for treated cell populations: technical replicates and biological cells define different observation units.

Likewise, 135 samples/day does not guarantee 135 independent biological replicates per day. Blanks, quality-control samples, reruns and allocation across experimental days affect study-level capacity. Conversely, dividing a day by the gradient duration alone would erase sample-loading time.

## The library is part of the measurement system

The earlier scpLASP study reported different identification counts when single-cell data were analyzed using an in silico library or a library generated from ten-cell samples. The relevant comparison is [Figure 7B of the earlier study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10999711/#fig7) ([2](#ref-2)). Identification depth therefore depends on the information supplied to search the data, as well as on the material entering the instrument.

This does not make library use inappropriate. Information from multicell samples can reasonably assist analysis of low-input material. Whether the same depth transfers to an unfamiliar cell state or drug-treated sample is a separate question. The abstract alone does not establish how far that transfer was tested in the new report.

Nor do I interpret the difference between 2024 and 2026 identification counts as the isolated improvement of one step. Instrumentation, LC conditions, cells and libraries may differ.

## Per-cell counts and shared detections

Consider a hypothetical comparison universe of \(N=8000\) protein groups. This is neither a claim about the true number of proteins in a human cell nor an estimate of the new workflow's accessible universe. Each item is detected in each cell with the same probability \(p\), independently across cells. Abundance-dependent and treatment-dependent detection are omitted.

Expected detections in one cell are \(Np\); expected shared detections in two cells are \(Np^2\). For \(m\) cells, the expected number observed in every cell is

\[
E[C_m]=Np^m.
\]

Independence among different protein groups is not required for this expectation. Linearity of expectation suffices if each group's across-cell detections satisfy the stated assumptions. Table 1 was generated by executing this formula.

Table 1. Expected detection counts in a hypothetical universe of 8000 items

| Per-cell detection probability \(p\) | One cell | Shared by two cells | Shared by all ten cells |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.50 | 4000.0 | 2000.0 | 7.8 |
| 0.75 | 6000.0 | 4500.0 | 450.5 |
| 0.90 | 7200.0 | 6480.0 | 2789.4 |

Entries are expected protein-group counts, hence fractional values. They are neither medians nor measurements from real cells.

At \(p=0.75\), 6000 detections per cell correspond to 4500 shared detections for a pair, on average. Requiring no missing observation in any of ten cells reduces the expectation to approximately 451. A large per-cell count does not ensure a large complete comparison matrix.

The same count of 6000 is compatible with another system that always detects exactly the same 6000 items. Its ten-cell intersection would remain 6000. Per-cell counts alone therefore do not determine the intersection. Altering the independent-detection assumption changes Table 1.

## A complete matrix is not the only analysis

Table 1 illustrates the cost of retaining only items detected in every cell. It does not recommend complete-case filtering. Models accommodating missing observations or within-group aggregation can use more information than the intersection. The observation model must correspond to the analytical question.

Replacing undetected values with zero is not a general solution either. Absence of detection can reflect low abundance or actual biological absence, with different meanings for the target comparison. If treatment changes cell size or total protein, total-signal normalization can also alter the biological difference retained in the analysis.

Real mass-spectrometric detection probabilities need not be equal across proteins; abundance and peptide properties can matter. Table 1 omits those dependencies and cannot predict missingness in the new report. It establishes only that identification depth leaves another quantity unresolved when the aim is comparison.

## More observations and the drug-research question

Deeper single-cell measurements expand the opportunity to observe states hidden in a population average. The new report alone does not establish improved drug-response prediction or fewer cells required for a study. Its stated result concerns a particular cell and measurement system; comparability across drug treatments remains outside what I could verify.

The calculation evaluates neither biological variation nor parameter uncertainty. It concerns detection expectations, with no fit to observed data or power analysis. Within that scope, **deeper measurement and more shared observations for comparing drug responses are different achievements**. Connecting 5655 to a drug-development judgment requires reading it alongside evidence about what is repeatedly measurable under comparable conditions.
