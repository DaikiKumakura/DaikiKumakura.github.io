Whether the target is B7-H3, DLL3 or SEZ6, are these drugs ultimately just delivering the same class of TOP1 inhibitor? An October 26, 2026 X post argued that different ADCs converge on median progression-free survival of 5–6 months and median overall survival of 12–14 months in second-line small-cell lung cancer ([1](#ref-1)). The implication went beyond similar numbers to question the value of changing targets.

Returning to the source tables reveals an earlier problem: the second-line numbers are not identical. Even within one drug's report, medians differ between the overall cohort and its second-line subgroup. Randomized comparisons against existing treatment have also been reported in 2026. Similar medians, equivalent treatment effects and identical mechanisms are separate claims.

## Scope and sources

I examined public reports available on November 1, 2026 for antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) carrying topoisomerase I (TOP1) inhibitor payloads and corresponding to the five targets named in the post. Second-line treatment generally means treatment after one prior line, but I did not harmonize each trial's eligibility rules or exceptions into a common definition.

The original post replies to STL Biotech, whose post quotes Hidehito Horinouchi's summary of IDE849 at ESMO 2026, poster 3815P ([2](#ref-2), [3](#ref-3)). I followed that chain and checked clinical papers, abstracts and developer disclosures. This is neither a systematic review of all trials nor an individual-participant analysis. It does not estimate comparative drug efficacy by meta-analysis.

Progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) retain each report's original analysis definitions. The same median time after treatment initiation does not imply the same survival curve or the same outcome without that treatment.

## Extracting the second-line results

Table 1 lists second-line results. In particular, the SEZ6 row represents 17 second-line patients receiving ABBV-706 at 1.8 mg/kg, not all 124 patients with small-cell lung cancer (SCLC). Matching the line-of-treatment label does not match dose, population, assessor or data cutoff.

**Table 1. Second-line results corresponding to the five targets in the post. Durations are in months; parentheses contain 95% confidence intervals.**

| Target and drug | Second-line patients | Median PFS | Median OS | Report |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| B7-H3: ifinatamab deruxtecan | 32 | 5.6 (3.9–8.1) | 12.0 (7.3–19.1) | IDeate-Lung01, 12 mg/kg ([4](#ref-4)) |
| SEZ6: ABBV-706 | 17 | 6.77 (4.01–12.45) | 14.26 (7.75–NE) | Phase 1, 1.8 mg/kg ([5](#ref-5)) |
| TROP2: sacituzumab govitecan | 43 | 4.40 (3.81–6.11) | 13.60 (6.57–14.78) | TROPiCS-03 ([6](#ref-6)) |
| EGFR/HER3: izalontamab brengitecan | 22 | 6.2 (3.7–8.2) | 15.0 (8.7–NR) | Phase Ib ([7](#ref-7)) |
| DLL3: IDE849 (SHR-4849) | 51 | 6.9 (5.5–8.3) | 17.4 (9.9–NR) | October 23, 2026 disclosure, doses ≥1.8 mg/kg ([8](#ref-8)) |

NE means not estimable and NR means not reached, preserving the source terminology. PFS was assessed by blinded independent central review for ifinatamab deruxtecan and by investigators for the selected ABBV-706 and TROPiCS-03 results. The intervals describe uncertainty in individual median estimates, not intervals for differences between drugs. EGFR is epidermal growth factor receptor; HER3 is human epidermal growth factor receptor 3.

The ifinatamab deruxtecan subgroup corresponds to [original Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12834294/#T4); ABBV-706 corresponds to [original Table 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04452-0/tables/3). The June 2026 correction to the latter paper concerns a reference, not the clinical outcomes used here ([4](#ref-4), [5](#ref-5), [9](#ref-9)).

Across these five reports, median PFS spans 4.40–6.90 months and median OS 12.0–17.4 months. These are the minimum and maximum disclosed estimates, not a pooled effect. Calling the numbers broadly similar is a possible description, but they do not all literally lie within 5–6 and 12–14 months. Their spread does not establish a drug ranking either.

## The population changes the number within one drug

All-line cohorts and second-line subgroups are easy to mix. Table 2 pairs results within the same report. For ABBV-706, I use the same 1.8 mg/kg dose in 41 patients and its 17-patient second-line subgroup, avoiding an additional change of dose.

**Table 2. Mixed-line cohorts and second-line subgroups in the same report. Durations are in months.**

| Drug | Mixed-line → second-line patients | Median PFS | Median OS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ifinatamab deruxtecan | 137 → 32 | 4.9 → 5.6 | 10.3 → 12.0 |
| ABBV-706 (1.8 mg/kg) | 41 → 17 | 6.44 → 6.77 | 12.42 → 14.26 |
| Izalontamab brengitecan | 52 → 22 | 4.1 → 6.2 | 12.2 → 15.0 |
| IDE849 | Disclosed all previously treated SCLC 101 → 51 | 6.8 → 6.9 | 14.7 → 17.4 |

Inputs come from the same sources as Table 1 ([4](#ref-4), [5](#ref-5), [7](#ref-7), [8](#ref-8)). The IDE849 release also reports 103 enrolled patients with SCLC, 101 response-evaluable patients and a denominator of 102 for overall survival-event reporting. Table 2 preserves the published population labels for the medians rather than assuming all these analysis sets coincide.

Simple subtraction gives second-line minus mixed-cohort median OS differences of 1.70, 1.84, 2.80 and 2.70 months, respectively. These are not treatment effects. Each subgroup overlaps its parent cohort, and treatment line was not randomly assigned. Testing these values as independent groups would be inappropriate. They nevertheless show that choosing a different cohort from one drug's report can move the apparent number by several months.

## Check the data version rather than the posting date

Horinouchi's post describes median PFS of 6.8 months, 12-month OS of 64.1% and median follow-up of 8.7 months for all SCLC ([3](#ref-3)). The October 23 IDE849 release specifies an August 21 cutoff and median follow-up of 12.1 months, reporting second-line median OS of 17.4 months ([8](#ref-8)). A survival percentage at 12 months cannot be read as a median survival time of 12 months. Differences between a post and an updated disclosure cannot be interpreted as a change in drug efficacy.

The other reports are not contemporaneous either. The ifinatamab deruxtecan paper uses a March 3, 2025 cutoff; ABBV-706 uses September 27, 2025; and the izalontamab brengitecan abstract reports data as of December 5, 2024 ([4](#ref-4), [5](#ref-5), [7](#ref-7)). Posts appearing together in 2026 do not necessarily describe equivalent observation periods.

## A median ceiling is not the same question as randomized benefit

Skipping the existing-treatment comparator would miss important evidence. The phase III TAISHAN-302 trial, reported in 2026, randomized 451 patients to the B7-H3 ADC tambotatug pelitecan or topotecan. Median OS was 13.3 versus 9.4 months, with a death hazard ratio of 0.46 (95% confidence interval 0.35–0.62), at the prespecified interim analysis ([10](#ref-10)). A median in the thirteen-month range can coexist with demonstrated benefit against the trial's control.

For another B7-H3 ADC, risvutatug rezetecan, developer disclosures of the phase III ARTEMIS-008 presentation report 461 randomized patients, median OS of 18.5 versus 10.3 months and a death hazard ratio of 0.46 (95% confidence interval 0.35–0.62) ([11](#ref-11)). I treat these as conference and developer-disclosed results, without claiming to have reviewed a peer-reviewed full paper. Identical displayed hazard ratios in two trials do not establish equivalence between the ADCs.

A single-arm median describes outcomes in a treated cohort; a randomized between-arm comparison provides different information. A hazard ratio also cannot generally be recovered by dividing medians. Median PFS and OS alone omit the tail of the survival curve, subsequent therapies and discontinuations or reductions due to toxicity. Looking for the longest median is insufficient to identify the benefit of changing an ADC.

## The results do not establish that the target is dispensable

Sharing a payload mechanism provides a reason to investigate common resistance or toxicities. The extracted PFS and OS estimates cannot, however, separate contributions from target binding, linker behavior, intratumoral delivery and exposure to released drug. Even superiority over a control would not establish that target-specific delivery explains the entire benefit. Conversely, a median close to another ADC's median does not demonstrate that the target is irrelevant.

For sacituzumab govitecan and izalontamab brengitecan, verification here centers on abstracts; TAISHAN-302 was checked through its original abstract; IDE849 and ARTEMIS-008 through developer disclosures. I did not reanalyze eligibility, imaging schedules, censoring rules or subsequent treatment under common definitions. Overlapping confidence intervals are not treated as evidence of equivalence, and no adjusted indirect drug comparison is constructed from aggregate values.

Whether SCLC ADCs share a biological limitation remains a valid question. These reports do not directly show that five drugs have reached the same ceiling. Second-line estimates still vary, and populations and data versions alter the numbers. Randomized evidence against existing treatment must also be counted before concluding that the development effort adds nothing.
