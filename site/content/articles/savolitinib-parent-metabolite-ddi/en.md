When a drug interaction increases parent-drug AUC, reducing the dose may bring that exposure back toward baseline. An active metabolite need not follow. A public savolitinib–fluvoxamine study provides a concrete example.

Here AUC means exposure from dosing to infinite time (AUCinf), distinguished from AUClast ending at the last measurable concentration. At the same 300-mg dose, the reported AUC ratios were 3.25 for savolitinib and 0.37 for its active metabolite M2. This analysis asks what exposure matching preserves. **Under a dose-proportional calculation that exactly matches parent AUC, M2 AUC becomes 11.4% of baseline.** That is not a prediction that efficacy becomes 11.4% of baseline.

## Question and scope

The QOI is whether a dose change matching parent AUC also preserves M2 exposure. The COU is a research calculation to examine which exposure measures need attention during a DDI, using public aggregate PK. It is not intended for prescribing, decisions about coadministration, or prediction of antitumor efficacy.

No new PopPK model or virtual-patient population is fitted. Aggregate results from 16 participants cannot recover the within-person parent–M2 correlations or the distribution of individual DDI effects. The useful calculation here is what exposure matching changes, rather than manufacturing a larger population from unavailable information.

## What the public study measured

The 2026 phase 1 study administered a single 300-mg savolitinib dose to 16 healthy male volunteers, followed in another period by the same dose during fluvoxamine 50 mg BID dosing. This was a fixed-sequence, within-person comparison, not a repeated-dose study in patients with cancer. M2 was described as active and M3 as inactive ([Miah et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.70321)).

| Measure | Parent | M2 | M3 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Same-dose AUCinf ratio | 3.25 | 0.37 | 3.27 |
| 90% CI of AUCinf ratio | 2.87–3.68 | 0.33–0.41 | 2.85–3.74 |
| Same-dose Cmax ratio | 1.95 | 0.12 | 1.58 |

These are geometric least-squares mean ratios from the original [Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13598373/), used directly below. Ratios recomputed from the separately reported geometric means differ slightly because of rounding and the analysis method.

## The exposure-matching calculation

Let \(d\) denote the dose multiplier, \(R_P\) the same-dose parent AUC ratio, and \(R_M\) the M2 AUC ratio. With dose-proportional PK, exposure during coadministration relative to baseline is

\[
E_P(d)=dR_P,\qquad E_M(d)=dR_M.
\]

Exact matching here refers to the aggregate parent AUC ratio, not matching every participant individually. Exact parent matching requires \(dR_P=1\), giving

\[
d=\frac{1}{R_P},\qquad E_M=\frac{R_M}{R_P}.
\]

The calculation assumes that the DDI ratios remain unchanged after dose reduction and that AUC scales with dose. The source describes approximate dose proportionality over 100–800 mg QD and 300–600 mg BID; the arithmetic 92.3-mg amount is below those ranges. This DDI study did not directly test that assumption below 300 mg. Applying the calculation to steady state is also unvalidated by this single-dose experiment.

## What remains different after parent matching

The following results are generated by `analysis/run.py`.

| Calculation | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Dose multiplier matching parent AUC | 0.3077 |
| Arithmetic amount obtained from 300 mg | 92.3 mg |
| Adjusted parent AUC ratio | 1.000 |
| Adjusted M2 AUC ratio | 0.1138 |
| Adjusted M3 AUC ratio | 1.0062 |

![Observed exposure ratios and arithmetic parent-matched ratios](shared/savolitinib-parent-metabolite-ddi/figures/exposure-matching.svg)

Blue bars are observed same-dose AUCinf ratios; orange bars are calculated under dose proportionality. Blue error bars are the original marginal 90% CIs, not intervals propagated to the orange bars. Orange bars are not measurements. The arithmetic 92.3-mg amount is not a marketed formulation or a recommended regimen.

Matching parent and M2 simultaneously would require \(dR_P=dR_M=1\). A single dose multiplier can therefore match both exactly only when \(R_P=R_M\). The observed changes go in opposite directions and do not meet that condition.

Combining the lower M2 CI endpoint with the upper parent endpoint, and vice versa, gives 0.0897–0.1429. This is a **sensitivity range formed from marginal CI endpoints**, not a 90% CI for the adjusted M2 ratio. A formal CI requires the covariance between the estimated log treatment contrasts, Cov(log R_M, log R_P), which was not reported. Individual prediction intervals additionally require the individual-level joint distribution of parent and M2 exposure.

## Before adding parent and M2 exposure

M2 is active, but simply adding AUCs in ng·h/mL ignores molecular weight, unbound fractions, relative potency and exposure at the site of action. The source discusses lower M2 potency, which alone does not identify its contribution in humans.

Let the unknown baseline M2 contribution to a weighted exposure index be \(f\). It is neither an estimated parameter nor a proportion of patients. I(f) is an illustrative index constructed from aggregate geometric-mean ratios, not the mean of individual parent-plus-M2 sums or a patient-level prediction. After exact parent-AUC matching, the normalized index becomes

\[
I(f)=(1-f)+f\frac{R_M}{R_P}.
\]

![Sensitivity of a normalized exposure index to an assumed M2 contribution](shared/savolitinib-parent-metabolite-ddi/figures/weight-sensitivity.svg)

For example, the index is 0.911 at \(f=0.10\) and 0.778 at \(f=0.25\). The entire horizontal axis is an assumption sensitivity analysis, not evidence that M2 contributes 25% in practice. If its contribution is small, the difference is small; if it is larger, matching parent exposure and matching the combined contribution diverge further. This equation does not predict response rates or toxicity rates.

## Why clearance and bioavailability are not estimated separately

For oral dosing,

\[
\mathrm{AUC}=\frac{FD}{CL},\qquad
R_P=\frac{F_1/F_0}{CL_1/CL_0}.
\]

Multiple combinations produce an AUC ratio of 3.25. Unchanged F with CL reduced to 0.3077 times baseline and a twofold increase in F with CL reduced to 0.6154 times baseline give the same ratio. The second is mathematically feasible if baseline F is sufficiently low. These examples do not estimate actual F.

In the source, parent terminal half-life changed from 8.9 to 8.0 h rather than increasing alongside AUC. Reported CL/F changed from 54.0 to 16.6 L/h and Vz/F from 693.1 to 191.6 L ([Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13598373/)). Treating a change in CL/F as a change solely in systemic CL loses this distinction.

For the same reason, formation and elimination rates were not forced onto a small set of aggregate parent and metabolite summaries. The algebraic model does not identify a mechanism; it restricts the analysis to exposure relationships calculable without uniquely identifying that mechanism.

## Interpretation

Matching parent AUC is not equivalent to matching M2 AUC. Public DDI ratios quantify that difference. Determining its clinical importance would require unbound M2 exposure and its functional contribution, repeated-dose PK in patients, and exposure–efficacy and exposure–safety relationships.

The original article also discusses label-based dose reductions. This arithmetic analysis neither replaces those recommendations nor validates them. Its independent research result concerns the **remaining metabolite-exposure difference even when parent exposure is matched**, rather than whether dose reduction should be prescribed.
