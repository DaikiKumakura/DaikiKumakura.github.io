## Definition

Flip-flop kinetics occurs when absorption after extravascular dosing is slower than elimination (ka < k). The terminal slope then reflects absorption, and the terminal half-life is the absorption half-life rather than the elimination half-life.

## How it is used

Subcutaneous depots and extended-release formulations are typical settings. Recognizing it matters because a model fitted without intravenous data can swap the two rate constants and misestimate V/F and the elimination half-life.

## Common pitfalls

- Reading a long terminal half-life after extravascular dosing as slow elimination without checking an intravenous profile or independent evidence.
- Predicting accumulation or washout from a terminal half-life that is actually governed by absorption.
