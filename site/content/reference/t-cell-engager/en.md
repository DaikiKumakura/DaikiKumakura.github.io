## Definition

A **T-cell engaging bispecific antibody (T-cell engager)** has two binding arms: one binds an antigen on the tumor cell and the other binds **CD3** on T cells. By bringing the two together, it activates T cells against the tumor cell without the need for the T cell's own antigen specificity.

## Examples

- **Mosunetuzumab:** CD20 × CD3, for B-cell lymphoma.
- **Tarlatamab:** DLL3 × CD3, for small cell lung cancer.

## Dosing issues

Strong T-cell activation can cause [cytokine release syndrome](cytokine-release-syndrome.html), most often after the first dose, so these drugs are usually given with [step-up dosing](step-up-dosing.html). Their dosing intervals are also chosen with [trough](ctrough-trough-concentration.html) and peak exposure in mind.
