## Definition

The body is treated as one well-mixed volume V with elimination clearance CL. The elimination rate constant is k = CL/V and the half-life is ln 2 / k. After an intravenous bolus, concentration falls mono-exponentially, so log concentration against time is a straight line. With first-order oral absorption (rate constant ka), C(t) = F · dose · ka / (V (ka − k)) × (exp(−k t) − exp(−ka t)).

## How it is used

It is the first model to try for sparse data, for dose-interval calculations and for teaching, because it has only two disposition parameters.

## Common pitfalls

- A visible bend in the semi-logarithmic plot (a fast early decline followed by a slower one) means a two-compartment model may be needed.
- V is a proportionality constant between amount and concentration, not a physical space.
