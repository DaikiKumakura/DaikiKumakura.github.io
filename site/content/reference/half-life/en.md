## Definition

The **half-life (t½)** is the time it takes for the concentration to fall by half in the terminal phase. For a one-compartment model with first-order elimination, **t½ = ln 2 × V / CL**: a larger volume lengthens it and a larger clearance shortens it. With several compartments there are several half-lives, and the **terminal half-life** is the slowest one.

## Why it matters

- **Steady state:** with repeated dosing of a drug with linear PK, steady state is reached after about four to five half-lives.
- **Washout:** the drug is nearly eliminated after the same number of half-lives.
- **Interval choice:** a dosing interval much shorter than the half-life accumulates, and one much longer gives large fluctuations between peak and trough.
