## Definition

A central compartment (volume V1) exchanges drug with a peripheral compartment (volume V2) at intercompartmental clearance Q, and drug is eliminated from the central compartment with clearance CL. After an intravenous dose the concentration falls bi-exponentially: a faster distribution phase, then a slower terminal phase. The volume of distribution at steady state is V1 + V2.

## How it is used

It is common for intravenous drugs, including antibodies, when early samples show a distinct distribution phase. Choosing between one and two compartments is usually judged by residual plots and objective-function comparisons.

## Common pitfalls

- Early samples that are too sparse cannot support the extra parameters; V2 and Q become poorly identified and strongly correlated.
- The peripheral compartment is an empirical description of distribution, not a named tissue.
