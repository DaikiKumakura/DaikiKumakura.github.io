## Definition

Sparse sampling collects only a few concentrations per patient, often one to four, at varying times relative to dosing. Population PK pools these records across patients and estimates typical values and variability together.

## How it is used

It suits studies where intensive sampling is impractical, such as large trials, children and patients who are ill. Sampling windows can be chosen by optimal design so that the pooled data cover absorption, the peak and the trough. Accurate records of actual dosing and sampling times are essential.

## Common pitfalls

- Trough-only designs weakly identify absorption and peak exposure unless early samples or prior information are added.
- Individual estimates from sparse data shrink toward the population mean, which limits diagnostics based on them; see eta shrinkage.
