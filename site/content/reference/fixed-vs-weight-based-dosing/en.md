A flat dose (also called a fixed dose) is the same dose given to every patient regardless of body weight; a weight-based dose scales with body weight (mg/kg). For antibodies, the two differ mainly at the ends of the weight distribution.

## Definition

- **Weight-based dosing:** the dose is proportional to body weight, for example 2 mg/kg.
- **Fixed (flat) dosing:** every patient receives the same dose, for example 200 mg, regardless of weight.

Many monoclonal antibodies moved from weight-based to fixed doses on the basis of population PK models that compared exposure. Near the middle of the weight distribution the two give almost the same exposure.

## Where they differ

Exposure depends on weight through clearance and volume, but not in proportion to weight. Fixed dosing therefore gives lower exposure in heavy patients and higher exposure in light ones; weight-based dosing does the opposite relative to the trial-tested range.

In a simulation of published models for pembrolizumab and nivolumab on an adult body-weight distribution, the share of patients whose steady-state trough was below the lower limit exceeded 10% at about 150 kg and above for pembrolizumab 200 mg (13.3%) and at about 100 kg and above for nivolumab 240 mg. Weight-based dosing was worse on the light side in the first interval. These are comparisons of exposure, not of effect.

## Which is better?

Neither is better in general. Fixed dosing is simpler to prepare and reduces dosing errors; weight-based dosing follows body size more closely. For an antibody the choice rests on population PK, on how flat the exposure–response relationship is, and on the weight range of the treated population.
