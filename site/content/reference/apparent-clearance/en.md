## Definition

After oral dosing, only the fraction F of the dose reaches the circulation. A model fitted to oral data alone therefore identifies the ratios CL/F (apparent or oral clearance) and V/F (apparent volume), not CL, V and F separately. Their units are L/h and L. Because AUC after a single dose equals F × dose / CL, it also equals dose divided by CL/F.

## How it is used

Population PK analyses of oral drugs report CL/F and V/F together with their covariate effects. A covariate effect on CL/F can reflect clearance, bioavailability or both; the model alone cannot say which.

## Common pitfalls

- Reading CL/F as true clearance. A formulation or food effect that lowers F raises CL/F without any change in elimination.
- Comparing CL/F between groups with different F (for example different formulations) as if it measured elimination.
