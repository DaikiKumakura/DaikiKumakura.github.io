## Definition

**Clearance (CL)** is the volume of plasma that is completely cleared of drug per unit time, in units such as L/h or L/day. It is the proportionality between elimination rate and concentration.

## What it determines

For linear PK, AUC after an intravenous dose is the dose divided by CL, and at steady state the average concentration is the dose rate divided by CL. Anything that raises CL, such as a higher body weight, lowers exposure for a fixed dose.

## Antibodies

Monoclonal antibodies are eliminated mainly by catabolism, and clearance can also include target-mediated elimination (see [target-mediated drug disposition](target-mediated-drug-disposition.html)). Some antibodies show a clearance that falls over time on treatment.

## On this site

The reimplemented pembrolizumab model has a typical clearance of 0.22 L/day with a body-weight exponent of 0.595, and the nivolumab model a clearance that decreases over time by up to about 26%; see the [anti-PD-1 analysis](../writing/fixed-vs-weight-dosing-en.html). The [mosunetuzumab reimplementation](../writing/mosunetuzumab-public-pk-en.html) uses a time-varying clearance as an empirical description.
