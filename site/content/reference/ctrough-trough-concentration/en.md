## Definition

**Ctrough** (trough concentration) is the drug concentration measured immediately before the next dose is given. At steady state it is written **Ctrough,ss** (or Cmin,ss): the lowest concentration within a dosing interval, reached when the previous dose has been eliminated as far as it will be before the next one.

## How it differs from other exposure measures

- **Cmax:** the highest concentration in the interval (the peak).
- **Cavg:** the average concentration over the interval, which equals the dose rate divided by clearance at steady state for linear PK.
- **Cmin:** the minimum concentration observed in the interval. It is usually close to Ctrough; Ctrough is defined as the pre-dose sample, Cmin as the lowest value.
- **AUC:** the area under the concentration–time curve over the interval.

## Why the dosing interval matters

If the interval is lengthened while the average exposure is kept the same, the peak rises and the trough falls. In the [simulation of anti-PD-1 regimens](../writing/fixed-vs-weight-dosing-en.html), 400 mg every 6 weeks of pembrolizumab had nearly the same average concentration as 200 mg every 3 weeks, but a lower trough. Whether the trough or the average concentration is the right criterion depends on the exposure–response relationship, which that analysis does not settle. The [tarlatamab analysis](../writing/tcell-engager-regimen-design-en.html) shows the same direction for an interval change at constant dose intensity.
