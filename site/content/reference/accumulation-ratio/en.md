## Definition

The accumulation ratio is a chosen exposure metric after repeated dosing divided by the same metric after the first dose: Cmax, Ctrough or AUC over a dosing interval. For linear, time-invariant PK, the AUC over one interval at steady state equals the AUC to infinity after a single dose, so accumulation is mainly a statement about peaks and troughs.

## How it is used

It checks whether observed multiple-dose exposure matches the prediction from single-dose data, and it helps choose an interval for a given half-life. A shorter interval relative to the half-life gives more accumulation.

## Common pitfalls

- State which metric the ratio refers to. Cmax, trough and AUC accumulate differently.
- A ratio that differs from the linear prediction can point to nonlinear or time-dependent PK, such as saturable clearance or enzyme induction, rather than to an error in the interval.
