## Definition

A pharmacokinetic drug interaction changes the exposure of one drug (the victim) through another (the perpetrator) by inhibiting or inducing metabolizing enzymes or transporters, or by altering absorption, for example through gastric pH or chelation.

## How it is used

Studies compare exposure with and without the perpetrator and report geometric mean ratios of AUC and Cmax with confidence intervals. FDA labeling language classifies inhibitors by the AUC increase they cause: weak, 1.25- to under 2-fold; moderate, 2- to under 5-fold; strong, 5-fold or more. Static calculations, mechanistic static models and PBPK models are used to decide which clinical studies are needed.

## Common pitfalls

- A large ratio is not automatically clinically relevant; that depends on the exposure–response relationship of the victim drug.
- Time-dependent inhibition, induction and transporter effects are easy to miss with in vitro data that assume simple reversible inhibition.
