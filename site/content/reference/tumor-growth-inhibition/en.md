## Definition

Tumor growth inhibition (TGI) models describe longitudinal tumor measurements, usually the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions, as growth plus a drug effect. Typical forms combine exponential or saturating growth with a drug-induced decline and, in some models, a resistance term that reduces the effect over time.

## How it is used

Model-derived metrics, such as tumor size ratio at a landmark week, time to tumor growth or the shrinkage rate, are linked to survival. Claret et al. (2009) used a phase II tumor-dynamics model to predict phase III overall survival. TGI models also support dose–response and exposure–response analysis with continuous data in place of response categories.

## Common pitfalls

- Early discontinuation, missing scans and new lesions make measurements informatively missing; the association with survival can be distorted.
- Immunotherapy can show atypical patterns, such as an initial increase followed by shrinkage, that standard growth-decay forms do not describe.
