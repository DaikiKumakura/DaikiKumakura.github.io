## Definition

Model-informed drug development (MIDD) uses quantitative models of data to inform a defined drug development or regulatory decision. The models include PK, exposure–response, PBPK, QSP and trial-simulation models.

## How it is used

Typical decisions are dose selection and regimen, trial design and sample size, extrapolation to children or special populations, and interpretation of labeling questions. ICH M15 gives general principles: state the question of interest and context of use, assess the risk of the model-supported decision from the model's influence and the consequence of an incorrect decision, and document model evaluation. In the US, the FDA runs a MIDD paired-meeting program.

## Common pitfalls

- Building a model before stating the decision and the alternatives it must separate.
- Reporting simulations without model performance and without propagating the uncertainty that matters for the decision.
