## Definition

The NOAEL is the highest dose in a toxicity study at which no adverse effect is observed, under the conditions of that study. It depends on the doses tested, the species, the duration and the endpoints measured, and is paired with the lowest observed adverse effect level (LOAEL).

## How it is used

For a first-in-human trial, the FDA's 2005 guidance converts the NOAEL from the most appropriate species to a human equivalent dose, usually by body surface area, and applies a safety factor, by default 10. For drugs with high risk, an approach based on the minimal anticipated biological effect level (MABEL) may be used. Exposure at the NOAEL, from toxicokinetics, is compared with expected human exposure as an exposure margin.

## Common pitfalls

- A NOAEL is limited by dose spacing; the true threshold may lie between tested doses.
- It is not a human threshold; compare exposure and findings across species.
