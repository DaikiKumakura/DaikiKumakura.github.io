## Definition

**Dose intensity** is the dose delivered per unit time, for example mg per week. **Relative dose intensity** is the dose intensity actually delivered divided by the planned one, and is used to describe delays and dose reductions.

## Constant dose intensity

If the dose is multiplied by the same factor as the dosing interval (for example 240 mg every 2 weeks and 480 mg every 4 weeks), the dose intensity is the same. For linear PK the [average concentration](auc-area-under-the-curve.html) at [steady state](steady-state.html) is also the same, since it is the dose rate divided by clearance. A longer interval raises [Cmax](cmax-peak-concentration.html) and lowers [Ctrough](ctrough-trough-concentration.html).

## On this site

The [tarlatamab analysis](../writing/tcell-engager-regimen-design-en.html) compares regimens at constant dose intensity and shows that the direction is the same across the range of the published fixed effects: the average is preserved, the peak rises and the trough falls. It asks how far matching the average alone can set an interval. The [summary-value analysis](../writing/tarlatamab-summary-pk-en.html) asks what the published values determine.
