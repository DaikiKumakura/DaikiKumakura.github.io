## Definition

A concentration–QTc analysis is a linear mixed-effects model that relates drug concentration to the change from baseline in the heart-rate-corrected QT interval, usually corrected for the time-matched placebo change (ΔΔQTc). Concentrations and ECG measurements are time-matched within each participant.

## How it is used

Under the ICH E14 and S7B Q&As it can serve as the primary assessment of QT risk in early-phase studies instead of a dedicated thorough QT study. The usual criterion is whether the upper bound of the two-sided 90% confidence interval of the predicted ΔΔQTc at the relevant concentration stays below 10 ms.

## Common pitfalls

- A delay between concentration and effect (hysteresis) can bias the slope; check an effect-compartment alternative.
- The correction formula, the concentration range achieved and any active metabolites determine what the model can support.
