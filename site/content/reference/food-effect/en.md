## Definition

A food effect is a change in the rate or extent of absorption of an orally administered drug when taken with food. Food can slow gastric emptying, raise the solubilization of lipophilic drugs through bile secretion and change splanchnic blood flow.

## How it is used

A typical study is a crossover of fed and fasted administration, usually after a high-fat, high-calorie meal. The geometric mean ratios of AUC and Cmax with 90% confidence intervals are reported; confidence intervals within 80.00–125.00% are generally taken as no clinically significant food effect. Labeling then states whether the drug should be taken with or without food.

## Common pitfalls

- Meal composition and the interval between meal and dose change the result; state them.
- Cmax and AUC can respond differently; a delayed peak with similar AUC can matter for some outcomes and not for others.
