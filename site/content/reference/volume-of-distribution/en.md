## Definition

The **volume of distribution (V)** is the amount of drug in the body divided by the plasma concentration. It is the volume that the body would need if the drug were at the plasma concentration everywhere. A drug that binds tissue strongly has a large V.

## Compartment models

In a two-compartment model, **V1** is the central volume (plasma and fast-equilibrating tissue) and **V2** the peripheral volume. **Vss** is the steady-state volume, V1 + V2 for a two-compartment model.

## Antibodies

Monoclonal antibodies have small volumes, of the order of a few liters for V1. In the published models re-implemented on this site, pembrolizumab has V1 = 3.48 L and V2 = 4.06 L, and nivolumab V1 = 3.63 L and V2 = 2.78 L (see the [anti-PD-1 analysis](../writing/fixed-vs-weight-dosing-en.html)).

## Relation to half-life

For a one-compartment model, the half-life is ln 2 × V / CL; see [half-life](half-life.html). The [tarlatamab analysis](../writing/tarlatamab-summary-pk-en.html) asks how well four parameters, including the volumes, can be identified from published summary values.
