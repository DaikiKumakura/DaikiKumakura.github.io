## Definition

In an **indirect response model** the drug does not produce the effect directly. It inhibits or stimulates the production, or the loss, of a **response variable** that is continually produced and lost (turns over), for example a clotting factor. The response variable's level follows the drug through its own turnover, so the **effect lags behind the concentration**.

## Four basic types

The drug can (1) inhibit production, (2) inhibit loss, (3) stimulate production or (4) stimulate loss, usually through an [Emax or Hill function](emax-hill-model.html) of the concentration (Dayneka, Garg and Jusko, 1993).

## Why it matters

Plasma concentration and effect are not in step. Peak effect comes after the peak concentration, and a short exposure can have a long effect. Fitting a direct Emax model to such data distorts the parameters.

## On this site

The [warfarin PK/PD analysis](../writing/warfarin-pkpd.html) uses a turnover-type response in which the turnover rate delays the response even when the plasma concentration changes promptly. The [degrader analysis](../writing/er-protac-pkpd-en.html) links a drug's PK to its effect on the target protein level through turnover, and asks whether maximal degradation alone can set the dosing interval.
